How to get clients on Instagram (coaches, agencies, freelancers)

To get clients on Instagram, make your profile say exactly who you help and show proof, post content your buyers care about, engage with them every day, and start short DM conversations that lead to a call. Inbound and outbound work together: content makes your DMs credible, and DMs put your content in front of the right people.

The short answer

  • Your bio should say who you help, with what, and how to start.
  • Post proof: results, examples, process and client stories.
  • Spend time daily engaging with the exact people you want as clients.
  • Start conversations with short, relevant DMs. Sell on a call, not in the inbox.
  • Track messages sent, replies, calls booked and clients won every week.

Set up the profile to convert

Every DM you send and every post that reaches a new person leads back to your profile. If it does not explain what you do in a few seconds, you lose the lead.

  1. Name field: your name plus your role or result, since it is searchable.
  2. Bio line one: who you help and with what. Line two: proof or a credential. Line three: what to do next.
  3. Link: one clear destination, such as a booking page or a short application.
  4. Highlights: results, testimonials, how it works, FAQs.
  5. Pinned posts: your best proof, your clearest explanation of the offer, and one personal post.

Post content that shows proof

You do not need to post every day. You need posts that a buyer could look at and think, this person can solve my problem. Client results with permission, before and after examples, breakdowns of how you work, answers to questions buyers ask on calls and short stories about specific clients all do that.

Content also supports outreach. When someone gets your DM and checks your profile, recent useful posts are what make them reply.

Engage with your buyers every day

Set aside 20 to 30 minutes a day to comment thoughtfully on posts from people you would like as clients, reply to stories and answer questions in your niche. Comments with an actual point get noticed; 'great post' does not.

This builds familiarity before you message, and it fills your own engagers list, which is the warmest source of leads. Our guide on finding leads on Instagram covers where else to look.

Start conversations in DMs

Reply to every comment and story reaction with a message. Welcome new followers who match your client profile. Then add a small amount of cold outreach to people who fit but have not found you yet.

Keep first messages short and about them, with one easy question. Move to a call only when there is a clear need. See how to write a DM that gets replies and the DM templates for examples.

What changes by business type

  • Coaches: lead with transformation stories and the specific situation you help with. Message people already showing the problem, such as those posting about starting a journey or following bigger coaches in your niche. Calls are usually a consult or discovery call.
  • Agencies: show work and numbers you can actually back up. Message business owners about something specific you noticed on their account or site, and offer a small, concrete idea rather than a full proposal.
  • Freelancers: your portfolio is your content. Post finished work and short process clips, and message brands, creators or businesses whose work is close to what you do best. Ask about their current project or bottleneck.

Turn replies into clients

  1. Answer their reply and ask one question about their situation.
  2. When a need is clear, suggest a short call and give two time options or one booking link.
  3. Confirm the call in the DM the day before.
  4. On the call, ask more than you talk, then make one clear offer.
  5. Follow up after the call within a day, in the channel they prefer.

Tip. Write down every question prospects ask in DMs. Those questions are your next posts.

Questions

How long does it take to get clients from Instagram?

It varies widely by offer, price and audience, and there is no reliable average. Consistent daily engagement and outreach usually produce conversations within the first few weeks; turning them into clients depends on your offer and calls.

Do I need a lot of followers to get clients?

No. A clear profile and good proof matter more than follower count for outreach. Prospects check whether you look credible, not whether you are famous.

Should I put my prices on Instagram?

Some people do, many prefer to discuss them on a call. If you work with very different scopes, a starting from price can filter out poor fits without boxing you in.

Is it okay to cold DM potential clients?

Messaging people is a normal feature. Keep it relevant and paced. High volume or repetitive messaging falls under Meta's spam policy.
