How much does an Instagram appointment setter cost?

An Instagram appointment setter is usually paid in one of four ways: hourly, base plus commission, commission only, or a flat fee per booked call. In the US, Payscale puts the average appointment setter base rate at about $16 an hour, while commission-based setter pay depends entirely on your offer price and close rate.

The short answer

  • Four common pay models: hourly, base plus commission, commission only, and per booked call.
  • Payscale: US appointment setters average about $16 per hour base, with most between about $13 and $21.
  • SetSmart, a setter platform, estimates beginner setters earn about $500 to $1,500 a month and experienced setters about $1,500 to $3,000.
  • Commission is usually tied to cash collected, not contract value.
  • Work out the cost per booked call and per client, not just the hourly rate.

What an appointment setter does

On Instagram, a setter starts and manages DM conversations, qualifies people, and books the ones who fit onto a call with you or your closer. In high-ticket coaching and agency sales this split is common: the setter fills the calendar, the closer runs the call.

Some setters only handle inbound replies and comments. Others also do cold outreach. Be clear which you are hiring for, because it changes the workload and fair pay.

The four pay models

  • Hourly or salary. Predictable for the setter, a fixed cost for you. Payscale reports a US average of about $16.08 an hour base (10th to 90th percentile roughly $13 to $21), from 76 profiles updated May 2026. This covers appointment setters in general, not only Instagram.
  • Base plus commission. A lower base plus a percentage of cash collected or a bonus per qualified call. The most common model for setters you want to keep long term.
  • Commission only. A percentage of cash collected from deals the setter booked. Cheap if nothing closes, but you attract less experienced setters and higher turnover.
  • Per booked or per showed call. A flat fee for each qualified call booked or attended. Easy to track, but define qualified carefully or you pay for poor calls.

What the published ranges say

There is no authoritative survey of Instagram setter pay. The best available figures are general setter salary data and estimates from companies in the setter space:

  • Payscale (salary data): about $16 per hour base for US appointment setters, with total annual pay including bonus and commission in a range of roughly $28,000 to $47,000.
  • SetSmart (setter platform, vendor estimate): beginner setters about $500 to $1,500 a month, experienced setters about $1,500 to $3,000. The same source puts closers at base plus 5% to 10% commission when starting and 10% to 20% when experienced. SetSmart does not publish where these figures come from.
  • Job postings: remote setter postings vary widely, from commission-only percentages of cash collected to flat fees per booked call. Treat earnings claims in postings as the employer's projection.

Tip. Setter commission is usually smaller than closer commission, because the closer carries the call that turns the booking into revenue.

Example math

These numbers are made up to show the calculation, not benchmarks. Say your offer is $3,000, your setter books 20 qualified calls a month, 70% show up, and you close 20% of those who show.

That is 14 calls held and about 3 clients, or $9,000 collected. At a 5% setter commission, the setter earns $450. Add a $1,000 monthly base and your total setter cost is $1,450, or about $72 per booked call and about $480 per client.

Run the same sum with your own numbers. If your offer is cheap or your close rate is low, commission-only pay will not attract anyone good. If your offer is high-ticket, a base plus commission usually works better than a high hourly rate.

Costs people forget

  • Training time: scripts, objections, your offer and how you qualify.
  • Management: reviewing conversations every week, especially early.
  • Tools: CRM or spreadsheet, scheduling, and any outreach software.
  • Account access: setters often work inside your Instagram account. Use proper shared access where available, never hand out passwords loosely, and stay in control of the account.
  • Contracts and tax: employee or contractor rules depend on your country and state. Check before you hire.

Questions

Should setter commission be based on cash collected or contract value?

Cash collected is more common and safer for you, because you only pay on money you actually received. If clients pay in instalments, agree whether commission follows each payment.

Should I pay per booked call or per showed call?

Per showed call protects you from no-shows but puts that risk on the setter. Many businesses pay on showed, qualified calls and define qualified in writing.

Are setters employees or contractors?

It depends on how you manage them and on local law. Rules differ by country and US state, so check with an accountant or employment lawyer.
