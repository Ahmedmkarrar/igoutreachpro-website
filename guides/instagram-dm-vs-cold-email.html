Instagram DM vs cold email: which should you use?

Use Instagram DMs when your buyers run their business on Instagram, such as coaches, creators, salons, cafes and local services. Use cold email when your buyers are B2B decision makers who live in their inbox, and use both when your audience spans the two.

The short answer

  • DMs fit audiences that are active on Instagram. Email fits B2B buyers who work from their inbox.
  • Instagram handles are visible on the profile. Emails often have to be found and verified.
  • Cold email needs domains, authentication and deliverability work. DMs need a credible, warmed-up account.
  • Email can scale to higher volume. Instagram DM volume is limited by unpublished account limits.
  • Email has published reply benchmarks (vendor data, roughly 1% to 6%). Instagram DMs have no reliable public benchmark.

The quick comparison

  • Where it lands. A DM from a stranger goes to Message Requests. A cold email goes to the inbox, promotions or spam depending on deliverability.
  • What the recipient sees first. On Instagram they see your profile, posts and followers. In email they see your name, subject line and a preview line.
  • Finding contacts. Instagram handles are public. Email addresses usually need research and verification.
  • Setup. DMs need an account that looks real and has some history. Cold email needs a sending domain, SPF, DKIM and DMARC, and warm-up.
  • Volume. Email can scale across multiple inboxes. Instagram limits DMs per account and does not publish the limits.
  • Rules. Cold email is governed by laws such as CAN-SPAM in the US and by inbox providers' sender rules. DMs are governed by Instagram's terms and Meta's spam policy.

When Instagram DMs are the better choice

DMs work best when your buyer's business is visible on Instagram: coaches, personal trainers, creators, beauty and wellness, restaurants, real estate agents, local service businesses and ecommerce brands. For these people Instagram is often where they answer customers, so a relevant message gets seen.

Your profile also does some selling for you. A prospect can see your posts, results and followers before replying, which is something a cold email cannot show at a glance.

When cold email is the better choice

Email wins when your buyers are managers, founders or executives at companies where Instagram is not part of the job: software, finance, manufacturing, professional services. Many of them rarely open Instagram for work, and a message there can feel out of place.

Email also handles more volume and longer messages, and it is easier to track at scale with established sales tools.

The rules for each channel

In the US, the FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide requires commercial emails to have accurate headers, honest subject lines, identification as an ad, a valid postal address and a working opt-out honoured within 10 business days, with penalties per violating email. Other countries have their own rules, some stricter.

Inbox providers add their own. Google's email sender guidelines require SPF or DKIM for all senders to Gmail, and senders over 5,000 messages a day also need DMARC and one-click unsubscribe for marketing mail. Google says to keep the spam rate below 0.10% and never reach 0.30%.

On Instagram, Meta's spam policy prohibits very high frequency or repetitive activity, and Instagram's terms restrict automated access. Automated DMs can conflict with those terms.

Reply rates: what the data says

For cold email there is plenty of vendor data. Hunter's 2025 report puts the average reply rate at 4.1%, and other vendors report averages from below 1% to around 6% depending on how they count. These figures come from companies selling email tools, so read them as rough ranges.

For Instagram DMs there is no reliable public benchmark. Instagram publishes nothing, and the numbers quoted in blog posts are usually unsourced. The only number that matters is your own: test 50 to 100 messages on each channel to the same type of buyer and compare.

Using both together

For many businesses the best answer is both. Email someone and engage with their Instagram content the same week, or DM first and move to email once they reply and want details. Touching two channels feels more human than five emails in a row.

Keep a single record of who you contacted and how, so nobody gets messaged on both channels at once by accident.

Questions

Do Instagram DMs get more replies than cold email?

There is no reliable public data comparing them. It depends heavily on the audience. Test both with the same buyer type and compare your own numbers.

Is cold email legal?

In the US, commercial email is legal if you follow CAN-SPAM. Other countries have different rules, and some require consent. Check the rules where your recipients are.

Which is cheaper to start?

Instagram DMs need less technical setup because you use an existing account. Cold email needs domains, inboxes and authentication before you send.
