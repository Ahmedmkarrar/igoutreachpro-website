How to find leads on Instagram

The best Instagram leads are people already showing interest in your topic: followers of accounts adjacent to yours, people posting on niche hashtags, commenters on posts in your space and people engaging with your own content. Find them there, then qualify each one from their profile before you message.

The short answer

  • Your own engagers are the warmest leads: commenters, story repliers and new followers.
  • Followers of adjacent accounts and niche hashtag posters are the biggest pools.
  • Commenters on niche posts are smaller pools with stronger intent.
  • Qualify from the profile: bio, recent posts, location, and whether they could plausibly buy.
  • Smaller, tighter lists usually get better reply rates than big broad ones.

Decide what a lead is first

Write one sentence describing who you want to talk to, specific enough that you could look at a profile and say yes or no in ten seconds. For example: independent hair salon owners in Texas with an active account, or business coaches with a paid program and under 20,000 followers.

Without that sentence you will collect thousands of handles and message the wrong people. With it, every source below becomes a filter instead of a firehose.

Where to find them

  • Your own engagers. People who commented, replied to a story, sent a reaction or recently followed you already know your name. Start here.
  • Followers of adjacent accounts. Pick accounts your ideal client follows but that do not compete with you: a supplier, a software tool, an event, a podcast in the niche.
  • Following lists. Who a well-known person in the niche follows is often a curated list of peers.
  • Niche hashtags. People posting on specific hashtags such as #salonowner or #newcoach are telling you what they do. Specific beats popular.
  • Commenters on niche posts. People asking questions under a post about your topic are actively thinking about it.
  • Location tags. Useful for local businesses: posts tagged at a specific venue, neighbourhood or city.
  • Your existing lists. Past customers, event attendees or newsletter subscribers who share their Instagram handle.

Qualify before you message

A large list is not the goal. In cold email data from Hunter, campaigns sent to 50 recipients or fewer averaged a 5.8% reply rate against 2.1% for campaigns sent to over 1,000. Email is a different channel, but the lesson carries over: relevance beats reach.

Check each profile quickly:

  1. Does the bio match your one-sentence lead description?
  2. Have they posted in the last month or so? Dormant accounts rarely reply.
  3. Is it a real person or business, not a meme page or obvious bot?
  4. Is there anything specific you could mention in a first message?
  5. Could they plausibly afford and need what you sell?

Tip. Note one detail per lead as you qualify. It becomes the first line of your message.

Keep a simple record

A spreadsheet is enough to start: handle, name, source, the detail you noticed, date messaged, follow-up dates, and outcome. The source column matters, because after a few weeks it tells you which pools actually reply.

Keep a do-not-contact column too. Anyone who says no or asks you to stop goes there and never comes back into a list.

Stay inside the rules

Instagram's Terms of Use prohibit accessing or collecting information in unauthorised ways, including collecting information in an automated way without its permission. Bulk scraping tools and bought lists of Instagram users carry real risk to your account, and bought lists are rarely relevant anyway.

Manual research, your own engagers and lists people gave you directly are the safest sources. If you use any automation, keep it light, and remember privacy laws where you and your leads live may also apply to how you store their details.

Turning a list into conversations

Questions

Should I buy a list of Instagram leads?

Generally no. Bought lists are usually untargeted, may be collected in ways Instagram's terms prohibit, and messaging uninterested people leads to declines and reports.

Are big hashtags good for finding leads?

Usually not. Very popular hashtags are full of unrelated posts. Narrow, specific hashtags tied to a job or situation give you far more relevant people.

How many leads do I need to start?

Fifty well-qualified leads are enough to test a message. Learn from those replies before building a bigger list.
